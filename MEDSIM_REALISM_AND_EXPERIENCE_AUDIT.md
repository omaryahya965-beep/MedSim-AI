## 1. Current project overview

MedSim AI is a single-room VR simulation. The student starts **inside** "Exam Room 3 · Resus" beside Dr. Sara Hassan (supervisor) and Mr. Adam Reyes (58, chest pain) lying in bed. The student interviews by push-to-talk voice or by tablet buttons, uses a stethoscope, BP cuff and pulse oximeter, records a 12-lead ECG, requests tests, explains their reasoning and receives a deterministic debrief.

| Layer | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Environment | One 6 × 5 m room generated by code (`Editor/MedSimSceneBuilder.cs`, 1,128 lines → 230+ generated meshes in `Generated/Meshes`) plus 10 Poly Haven CC0 props |
| Characters | MakeHuman / MPFB2 CC0 humans (Blender-generated), Humanoid rig, ARKit + viseme blend shapes, procedural "life" layer |
| Interaction | XR Interaction Toolkit 3.7 starter rig (controllers, teleport, snap turn), grab interactables, trigger-collider placement zones |
| Clinical engine | Pure C# `ClinicalSession`: scenario JSON, state machine, patient state, action tracker, rubric scoring |
| AI | `ConversationOrchestrator` → offline scripted provider (default) or grounded proxy (`Backend/medsim-proxy/server.mjs`, OpenAI) |
| Voice | Push-to-talk → Windows dictation (offline, PC only) or proxy STT; pre-rendered Windows SAPI voices (58 WAVs) or proxy TTS |
| UI | World-space menu board, bedside tablet on a pole (5 tabs), subtitles, "Listening / Thinking / Speaking" tag, debrief panel |

## 2. Actual implementation status

### 2.1 Fully functional (verified in this audit)
- **Deterministic clinical engine.** Scenario loading and validation, the 8-stage state machine, patient state (changed only by authored transitions), event log, rubric scoring, and the review gate that keeps the DRAFT banner. Covered by 52 passing EditMode tests.
- **Instrument → engine pipeline (scripted).** Placing the cuff and oximeter produced `EXAM:EX_BP` and `EXAM:EX_SPO2`. Pressing ECG Record produced `INV:INV_ECG`. The monitor revealed 152/94 and SpO₂ 96 from scenario data (`P05`, `P06`).
- **Patient monitor display.** Live HR, Pleth and Resp traces. NIBP and SpO₂ are hidden until measured (`P06`).
- **12-lead ECG rendering.** A schematic, scenario-keyed tracing on the cart screen and printout (`P07`).
- **Offline conversation.** Keyword-matched history questions return approved patient answers with pre-rendered audio and lip-sync.
- **Safety layers.** `InputGuard`, response validation, and server-side structured-output grounding. Unit-tested with fakes.

### 2.2 Partially functional
- **Voice input.** Offline it depends on Windows dictation, so it works on PC only, in English only. It has not been tested with the headset microphone. On Android it needs the proxy.
- **Characters.** They breathe, blink, gaze, lip-sync approximately and gesture procedurally, but they have **no animation clips and no Animator controllers** (both Animators: `ctrl=NONE`). Nobody can walk.
- **Supervisor coaching.** Fixed progress lines, hints on request and replies when addressed. There is no observation or intervention logic (see §8).
- **Reasoning assessment.** `SUMMARY_GIVEN` is awarded for **any** utterance of 8 or more words in the reasoning stage. `expectedReasoning.reasoningKeywords` is defined in the JSON but **never used** in code, so the *content* of the student's reasoning is not assessed.
- **Escalation detection.** Any supervisor-directed sentence containing an escalation keyword counts as escalation. The keywords include "stemi" and "heart attack", so "Is this a STEMI?" said to Dr. Hassan is scored as *escalated*.

## 3. Current scenario walkthrough (start → debrief)

1. **Start (t = 0).** 2.5 s loading. The student stands **inside the exam room** at the spawn point (1.2, 0, 0.65) with a 1.65 m eye height (+0.40 m default lift).
2. **Main menu.** A **floating navy menu board** hangs in mid-air in front of the bed (`P00`). It offers Start Clinical Session, Training Mode, Voice & AI Settings, Comfort & Graphics and Exit, plus a controls list.
3. **Onboarding.** Dr. Hassan speaks **four consecutive lines**: welcome, role, controls tutorial and instrument locations. **Measured: about 46 s of continuous speech before the student can act.** The controls line ("Hold the grip button…") breaks immersion.
4. **Communication.** The student looks at a person; `VoiceInteractionManager` auto-targets whoever is closest to gaze. They hold A/X to talk. Offline, Windows dictation transcribes the speech. Alternatively they press one of the preset question buttons on the **tablet** (`P04`). The patient's answer is the authored fact chosen by **substring keyword matching** (e.g. "describe" + "pain" → H_CHARACTER).
5. **Instruments.** The student grips an instrument on the trolley; every valid zone glows faintly. The **cuff and oximeter snap** when released on an accepted zone; the cuff runs a 7 s inflate/deflate animation. The **stethoscope** must dwell 1 s on a chest zone and plays procedural heart or breath sounds. **ECG:** the student presses the RECORD button on the cart, and the trace appears immediately.
6. **Results.** Every result comes from `ClinicalSession.PerformExam` / `RequestInvestigation` and is read from the scenario JSON. A finding toast is published to the tablet's Findings tab.
7. **Mistake detection.** **None.** Releasing an instrument on a wrong zone simply does nothing; it is not recorded. There is no idle detection, no ordering check and no safety-checkpoint logic.
8. **When the doctor speaks.** (a) the onboarding lines; (b) in **training mode only**, three one-time nudges: after ≥10 key history facts, after BP + SpO₂ + heart auscultation, and after the ECG; (c) a reasoning prompt when the stage changes to reasoning; (d) hints when the student asks; (e) a reply when addressed. There is no cooldown or priority system, because there is almost nothing to prioritise.
9. **How the AI receives context.** Only when the student speaks. The proxy receives scenario id, version, stage, revealed EXAM/INV ids and the last 8 turns. It never receives a stream of actions.
10. **Reasoning.** The student presses "I'm ready to summarise" on the tablet, or the stage changes. They speak or tap one of the preset "Summary:" buttons. Any utterance of 8 or more words scores `SUMMARY_GIVEN`.
11. **End.** "Finish case & debrief" on the tablet. Dr. Hassan says one line, and a **text debrief panel** shows scores, missed safety-critical items, the ECG time target, teaching points, the diagnosis and references. Results are saved to `persistentDataPath/medsim_sessions.json`.

**Scoring.** 21 rubric items (13 history, 4 examination, ECG, ECG review, escalation, summary). Observable and communication scores are kept separate. Three items are safety-critical (allergy, ECG, escalation). One timed target: ECG within 600 s.

**Practical improvement path (in order of value per effort):**
1. **Body animation first.** Add an Animator with real mocap clips: idle, idle-with-weight-shift, talk gestures, listen, walk, turn, stop, open door, and lying-in-bed idle and discomfort for the patient. 2. **Correct clothing.** A real lab coat and scrubs for the doctor and a real hospital gown for the patient. Either from a character pack that already has medical outfits (e.g. Microsoft Rocketbox includes medical professionals; see §11) or modelled in Blender.
3. **Better base humans** if the budget allows. Reallusion Character Creator / ActorCore, or Rocketbox (free, MIT). Choose one look for both characters.
4. **Doctor locomotion.** NavMesh (the AI Navigation package is already installed), plus root-motion walk clips, plus Animation Rigging look-at and hand IK to the door handle. Hand IK is optional.
5. **Lip-sync.** Replace text-timed visemes with **uLipSync** (MIT; audio-based, works with streamed TTS audio) or a phoneme-timed solution.
6. **Skin and eyes.** A URP skin shader with subsurface approximation, eye wetness/refraction, and hair cards with proper alpha. Lower priority than 1–4.

## 8. Current AI architecture

### 8.1 How it works today
```
Student speech (push-to-talk) ─► STT (Windows dictation offline | proxy /v1/stt)
Tablet button ────────────────►┐
                               ▼
ConversationOrchestrator.HandleAsync(target, utterance)
  1. Deterministic tracking: ProcessStudentQuestion (keyword → FACT:*) or
     ProcessInstructorStatement (keyword → INV:*, ESCALATION)
  2. InputGuard (prompt injection, state change, diagnosis, dosing, real-patient advice)
  3. Investigation results spoken from scenario data only
  4. Provider: OfflineScriptedProvider (default) or GroundedProxyProvider (Live AI)
       Proxy → OpenAI Chat Completions with Structured Outputs:
         patient   → {factIds ∈ approved enum (max 2), leadIn, offTopic}
         supervisor→ {intent ∈ enum, investigationId, mentionsEscalation, reply}
  5. Clinical text rendered locally from approved JSON; free text validated (no numbers, diagnosis, dosing, citations)
  6. AI-only recognitions tagged AI_INTENT and labelled in the debrief
TTS: pre-rendered SAPI WAV if the text matches exactly, else proxy /v1/tts
```

Instruments do **not** go through the orchestrator. They call `ClinicalSession` directly. The supervisor's unprompted speech comes from three hard-coded checks in `SessionManager`.

### 8.2 Can it support an intelligent supervising doctor?
**The foundation, yes. The behaviour, no.**
- **Strengths to keep:** the event log (`ClinicalActionTracker.Recorded` already fires for every clinical event), the authoritative scenario, the grounding contract (the AI selects; the scenario supplies the facts), and the offline fallback.
- **Missing:**
  1. **Events for wrong or incomplete actions.** No event exists for a wrong instrument/zone, removing the cuff before the reading, idle time, skipping consent before examining, or reaching a checkpoint.
  2. **A supervisor decision layer.** Nothing evaluates "should I speak now?" There is no priority, grace period, cooldown, repeat limit, or difference between an ordinary and a safety-critical mistake.
  3. **Authored intervention rules** in the scenario JSON. Today there are only 6 stage hints and 3 progress lines.
  4. **Reasoning evaluation.** The content of the student's explanation is never compared with the expected reasoning.
  5. **Streaming voice.** The pipeline is batch STT → classify → TTS, about 2–4 s per turn (estimated, never measured), and the TTS endpoint shuts down on 2027-01-06.

### 8.3 Recommended simple architecture (event-driven, no per-frame AI)
```
Student action/utterance
  → Unity records a structured ClinicalEvent (existing tracker + ~8 new event types)
  → SupervisorDirector (NEW, deterministic, C#): evaluates scenario-authored intervention rules
       • trigger (event pattern / missing event after N s / checkpoint)
       • severity: ordinary | safety_critical
       • grace period (ordinary mistakes: let the student self-correct)
       • priority + global cooldown (e.g. ≥ 20 s between unprompted remarks) + max repeats
       • escalating hint ladder: question → nudge → explicit (from approved lines)
  → If an intervention fires: AI (optional) picks the best approved hint level and phrases a
       short Socratic sentence through the existing validator; offline uses the authored line verbatim
  → Doctor speaks (spatial audio, gesture, gaze toward the relevant object)
Student explanation (Scene 6)
  → AI maps the free text to the scenario's reasoning concepts (finding ids, working-diagnosis concept,
       next-step concept) with a confidence score. The deterministic rubric scores only high-confidence
       matches; everything else is labelled "AI-interpreted — for instructor review"
```
- **Cost control:** AI is called only on (a) student speech, (b) a fired intervention when Live mode is on, and (c) the end-of-case reasoning evaluation. That is about 20–40 calls per session, not per frame.
- **Deterministic first:** the event, rule and timing logic is plain C# and unit-testable, so it works offline and on Android.

### 8.4 Is the existing OpenAI integration sufficient?
It is sufficient for **grounded classification** of patient questions and supervisor intents, which is the right design. It is **not** sufficient for natural voice or for supervision:
- TTS on `/v1/audio/speech` ends on **2027-01-06**. OpenAI names `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini` as the replacement, but that model is **not** supported on the speech endpoint, so this means a workflow change.
- The Realtime API (`gpt-realtime-2.1` / `-mini`, WebSocket or WebRTC) gives low-latency speech-to-speech and server-side voice activity detection. **Caution:** a free-speaking realtime model *generates* its own words, which conflicts with "the AI must never invent medical findings". The safe pattern is to use Realtime for **transcription plus tool calls** (e.g. `select_fact(factId)`, `request_investigation(id)`, `give_hint(level)`) and to have it **speak only the approved text returned by the tool**, or to use realtime transcription plus a separate TTS. This needs a prototype before committing.
- Model names in `AI_SETUP.md` (`gpt-6-luna`, `gpt-transcribe`) were not re-verified in this audit.

### 8.5 What is needed to activate genuine AI voice (checklist)
1. Your approval to make billable calls, an OpenAI API key with billing, and a spending limit set in the OpenAI dashboard.
2. Run `Backend/medsim-proxy` on the PC with `OPENAI_API_KEY` and `MEDSIM_CLIENT_TOKEN` (see `AI_SETUP.md`). Create `medsim_ai_config.json` with `preferOnline: true`.
3. Smoke test: `GET /health`, then 5 patient questions and 3 supervisor turns. Record latency and transcription accuracy, including Palestinian Arabic if needed.
4. Before 2027-01-06: replace `/v1/tts` (Realtime-based speech, or another TTS provider), or rely on pre-rendered audio for all approved lines. Approved lines are finite, so **pre-rendering every approved line with a high-quality neural voice** is the cheapest and most natural option for the demo. Live AI is then needed only for understanding, not speaking.
5. Headset test: microphone level, push-to-talk timing and VBS audio routing.

**Live AI has never been tested against a real provider.** Nothing in this report claims it works.

## 9. Medical grounding and safety

The project is unusually well grounded. Keep all of the following:
- Every patient fact, vital sign, exam finding, investigation result, hint and debrief point comes from `Resources/Scenarios/ER_CHEST_PAIN_001.json`. Patient state changes only through authored `transitions`, and the patient exposes defensive copies.
- The AI never receives the rubric, the diagnosis before feedback, or unrevealed results. It returns enum-constrained ids. Free text is validated on both server and client. AI-only recognitions are labelled in the debrief.
- `IsClinicallyValidated` requires `status == APPROVED` **and** reviewer name, credentials and date. The DRAFT banner is shown on the menu, tablet, posters and debrief.

Weaknesses to fix (accuracy of *assessment*, not of content):
1. Escalation keywords include diagnostic words ("stemi", "heart attack"), so naming the diagnosis to the supervisor counts as escalating. Separate *diagnosis named* from *escalation requested*.
2. `SUMMARY_GIVEN` rewards length, not content. Use `reasoningKeywords` (deterministic) plus AI concept-mapping labelled as uncertain.
3. Keyword matching can over-credit, e.g. a question containing "allerg" in another context. Show the matched utterance in the debrief; it is already logged in `detail`.
4. New intervention rules and lines must go into the same JSON and the same clinician review (`CLINICAL_REVIEW.md` items 6 and 11). **Do not invent procedures.** Use only the existing objectives and rubric to trigger interventions.

Status remains **DRAFT — NOT CLINICALLY VALIDATED**.

## 12. Simple, prioritised improvement plan

Each phase ends in a demonstrable state. The order maximises realism gained per hour and keeps the project small.

### Phase 0 — Quick fixes (½–1 day, no new assets)
1. Fix or remove the invisible chair, stool and empty fire alarm (re-import at the correct scale, or delete).
2. Replace the two rusty street bins with simple clinical pedal bins, and move them away from the door.
3. Add colliders to the bed frame, cabinets, chair and both characters (body capsule).
4. Give the window an exterior image (or frosted glass) instead of white.
5. Cut the onboarding to one ~10 s line; drop the controls monologue (put controls in the pause menu).
6. Separate the "diagnosis named" and "escalated" keywords; score the reasoning summary with `reasoningKeywords`.

### Phase 1 — Corridor and door (2–4 days)
7. Generate a short corridor in the same scene with the existing builder style, plus 2–3 good props (bench, signage, handrail, doors to other rooms that stay closed).
8. Add the bilingual Birzeit welcome sign above the exam room door (Noto Naskh/Kufi Arabic + `ArabicShaper`). Note: real institutional branding should be approved by the university.
9. Add an interactive hinged door with lever handle, latch, sound and collider. Fire an event when it opens.

### Phase 2 — Characters that move (4–6 days)
10. Choose the character source (§11): Rocketbox (free) or Character Creator (paid). Fix clothing (lab coat or scrubs; real gown).
11. Animator controllers + mocap clips: doctor idle, talk, listen, walk, turn, point; patient lying idle, discomfort, hand to chest, flinch, nod.
12. NavMesh + doctor waypoints: welcome in the corridor → walk with the student → enter → stand at the foot of the bed, observing.
13. uLipSync for audio-driven lip-sync.

### Phase 3 — The intelligent supervisor (5–8 days; the most important feature)
14. Add event types: `INSTRUMENT_PICKED`, `ZONE_REJECTED` (wrong zone), `INSTRUMENT_REMOVED_EARLY`, `EXAM_BEFORE_CONSENT`, `IDLE`, `CHECKPOINT_REACHED`, `DOOR_OPENED`, `STUDENT_REQUESTED_HELP`.
15. Add `interventions[]` to the scenario JSON (trigger, severity, grace, priority, cooldown, max repeats, hint ladder of approved lines). Send it for clinical review. Derive the triggers only from existing objectives and rubric items, e.g. "ECG not requested by 300 s" (the safety-critical timing already exists at 600 s), "examining before consent", "allergies not asked before the escalation discussion".
16. Implement a deterministic `SupervisorDirector` with unit tests: stays silent on correct actions, waits out the grace period on ordinary mistakes, intervenes immediately on safety-critical rules, never repeats within the cooldown.
17. Optional Live AI: choose the hint level and phrase it within the validator.

### Phase 4 — Discussion, debrief and voice quality (3–5 days)
18. Doctor-led clinical discussion (four questions), concept matching, uncertain items labelled for the instructor.
19. Spoken debrief by Dr. Hassan, with the detailed report as optional.
20. Re-render all approved lines with a natural neural voice (Arabic and English) to replace SAPI. Validate Live AI with your approved key; plan the TTS replacement before 2027-01-06.

### Phase 5 — Equipment and room polish (3–5 days, in parallel with 2–4 if someone else is available)
21. Swap in modelled assets: bed (first), BP cuff, ECG cart + electrode set, stethoscope, oximeter, monitor housing, chair, bins, medical cabinet, blanket.
22. Re-bake lighting; check performance on the headset; build the APK only after PC sign-off.

### Not recommended (keeps the project focused)
Multiple scenarios, multiplayer, a large hospital map, hospital-management features, or a rewrite of the clinical engine or the AI grounding layer. They all work and are well tested.
